Slow Down on Urgency

Real emergencies rarely require an instant, unverifiable payment. Pressure is itself a warning sign.

See All Red Flags

Nearly every scam on this page relies on the same mechanism underneath the specific story: manufactured urgency that pressures you to act before you have time to think it through or verify it independently. That pressure isn't incidental to the scam — it's the actual mechanism that makes the scam work, because a calm, unhurried person checking the facts is far harder to con.

This means urgency itself is worth treating as a signal, almost independent of the specific story attached to it. A request that insists you act in the next few minutes, that discourages you from telling anyone else, or that frames any delay as dangerous or costly deserves more scrutiny, not less — precisely because that's what it's designed to prevent.

In practice, this means building in a deliberate pause as a habit: for any request involving money, credentials, or a major decision, give yourself a fixed amount of time — even just ten minutes — to step away and think, regardless of how urgent it's framed. Legitimate situations can absorb that pause; scams generally cannot.

  • The pattern: Urgency plus a request for money or credentials, together, almost always signal a scam.
  • In practice: Give yourself a fixed pause — even ten minutes — before acting on any urgent financial request.
  • Red flag: Being discouraged from telling anyone else or taking time to think.
  • Remember: A real emergency can survive you taking a minute to verify it.
