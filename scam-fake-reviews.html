Fake Reviews & News

AI-generated reviews, testimonials, and fake news articles used to make a scam look legitimate.

See All Red Flags

Before a scam asks for money, it often needs to look credible first — and AI text generation makes it cheap to produce hundreds of realistic-sounding reviews, testimonials, or even fabricated news articles that appear to validate a fake product, investment platform, or company. Volume creates the illusion of consensus.

These show up padding out a scam site's own reviews section, seeded across review platforms, or as fake "as seen on" news articles designed to be screenshotted and shared as supposed proof of legitimacy — a tactic that works especially well because most people don't click through to verify the original source.

The fix is simple but underused: verify claims at the source. A news mention should be checkable directly on the actual outlet's site, not just trusted because a screenshot looks right. Reviews that are uniformly glowing, oddly similar in phrasing, or appeared all at once are worth treating with real skepticism.

  • The giveaway: Reviews that are uniformly glowing, similarly phrased, or all posted around the same time.
  • What to do: Verify any news claim directly on the outlet's actual site, not from a screenshot.
  • Red flag: Testimonials with no verifiable identity behind them.
  • Remember: Volume of praise is not the same as evidence.
